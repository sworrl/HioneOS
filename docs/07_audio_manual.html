settings list global | grep vendor.audio.hiby

The DAC and the SQ / HR badge

The M500 uses two Cirrus Logic CS43198 chips. HiBy Music tags each track with a badge based only on sample rate:

SQ lossless below 88.2 kHz  ·  HR lossless at 88.2 kHz or above (plus DSD).

Bit depth does not move the badge. A 24-bit / 48 kHz file is lossless and still reads SQ, because 48 kHz sits under the 88.2 kHz line. The badge measures the container's sample rate, not the fidelity.

Digital filter

vendor.audio.hiby.digital_filter   now: fast_rolloff_low_latency

The DAC's reconstruction filter, working just under 22 kHz. It shapes pre- and post-ringing and the last sliver of treble. It does not change tone or detail across the band, and the differences between the five are small.

The five options

  • fast_rolloff_low_latency: steep cut, minimum phase, no pre-ringing. Tight and immediate. Good default.
  • fast_rolloff_phase_compensated: steep cut, linear phase, symmetric ringing. Measures cleanest.
  • slow_rolloff_low_latency: gentle cut, minimum phase, less ringing, slightly softer top end.
  • slow_rolloff_phase_compensated: gentle cut, linear phase, symmetric ringing.
  • nos: no oversampling filter. Rawest; some rolled-off highs.

If cymbals or sibilance tire you, move to slow_rolloff_low_latency or nos. There is no wrong choice here.

settings put global vendor.audio.hiby.digital_filter slow_rolloff_low_latency

Oversampling (hardware NOS)

vendor.audio.hiby.hw.digital_filter   now: nos

A hardware non-oversampling toggle, separate from the software filter above. When it is set to nos the DAC runs without oversampling. Treat this and the software filter as one decision rather than two.

DRE (Dynamic Range Enhancement)

vendor.audio.hiby.dre_mode   now: dremode_disable

A CS43198 feature that raises the DAC's measured dynamic range with extra processing. On this unit it made 192 kHz / 32-bit playback stutter; turning it off cleared the stutter.

Leave it off. On adds processing and can glitch the highest-rate files. Off sounds a little more natural and plays hi-res clean. Try it on only for heavily compressed pop if you want a touch more body, then turn it back off for hi-res.

Output mode / gain

vendor.audio.hiby.high_power   now: hpower_enable (high gain)

Gain sets how much voltage swing is on tap. It is not volume and not quality.

  • Low gain for sensitive IEMs. Lower noise floor, finer volume steps, less hiss.
  • High gain for headphones that need more voltage to open up.

Use the balanced 4.4 mm jack when your gear supports it. It delivers more power than the 3.5 mm and usually a lower noise floor.

What the Miku IEMs want, and the math behind it

Worked for the Moondrop Aria (Hatsune Miku edition): 122 dB/Vrms at 32 Ω. That rating means 1 Vrms across the driver makes 122 dB SPL, past the threshold of pain. Solve it backward for real levels:

SPL = 122 + 20 · log10(Vrms)
Vrms = 10^((SPL − 122) / 20)
Listening levelVoltage at the driver
80 dB SPL (normal)7.9 mVrms
90 dB SPL (loud)25 mVrms
100 dB SPL (peaks)79 mVrms

The 3.5 mm output tops out near 1.97 Vrms, from V = √(P · R) = √(0.121 · 32) on the 121 mW / 32 Ω rating. So 80 dB listening uses 7.9 mV out of 1.97 V, which is 48 dB below full scale:

20 · log10(0.0079 / 1.97) = −48 dB

Two results follow, both pointing at low gain:

  • Noise floor. The single-ended path is rated 122 dB SNR against full output, so its noise sits near 1.97 · 10^(−122/20) = 1.6 µVrms. Through a 122 dB/Vrms IEM that noise reads 122 + 20 · log10(1.6×10⁻⁶) ≈ 6 dB SPL of hiss. In a quiet room with sealed tips, 6 dB SPL is audible. Low gain lowers the amp's output-referred noise, so the hiss drops.
  • Volume resolution. Sitting 48 dB down the range on high gain wastes the top of the control, and if the M500 attenuates in the digital domain it discards bits at your listening level. Low gain moves your band up into finer steps.
For the Aria Miku: low gain. The 4.4 mm balanced output (480 mW, 125 dB SNR, 3 dB quieter than SE) is worth using for the lower noise and better channel separation, not for the wattage, which this IEM never needs. High gain on this DAP is for full-size headphones. If yours are the CHU II Miku instead (119 dB/Vrms, 18 Ω), the voltages shift a little but the answer holds: low gain.

Output routing

vendor.audio.hiby.hw.bal_po_lo_switch   now: bal_po (balanced phone out)

Selects balanced phone out, single-ended phone out, or line out. Line out is a fixed level for feeding an external amp or dock, and it bypasses the internal volume. For headphones and IEMs, keep it on phone out.

Amp bias (class)

vendor.audio.hiby.hw.balpo_po_a_ab   now: zoom_ab (Class AB)

The internal amp bias. The M500's amp runs Class AB. The pricier R5 and R6 ship with a user Class-A switch; the M500 does not, so treat this as fixed unless a matching menu toggle turns up.

Digital output format

vendor.audio.hiby.hw.iisspdif   now: spdif

The format on the data pin for digital output, I2S against SPDIF. It matters only when you use a digital or coax output. For headphone listening it does nothing audible.

CPU governor for audio

vendor.audio.hiby.cpu_tuner   now: performance

The CPU governor the audio path requests. performance pins the cores near their top clock, which lowers latency and jitter at a small cost in battery.

Reading and changing settings

TaskCommand
Read allsettings list global | grep vendor.audio.hiby
Read onesettings get global vendor.audio.hiby.digital_filter
Change onesettings put global vendor.audio.hiby.<key> <value>

The shell holds WRITE_SETTINGS, so a write succeeds and reads back. Whether the audio driver re-reads a change live, or only at boot or on the next playback start, is not yet confirmed on this unit. When you are unsure, change the setting from the on-screen menu, or restart playback after a write. The authoritative values live in the global namespace; a write to system makes a stray copy the driver ignores.
